DEVIATIONS
----------
Two, both in the brief's stated rationale and verification rather than in
what ships. NO CHANGE TO THE SHIPPED MAKEFILE RESULTED FROM EITHER.

DEVIATION 1 — §6.3's ceiling was asserted against the wrong figure.
  §6.3 as written required `docker system df` Build Cache SIZE at or below
  5GB after the sweep. Observed 5.781GB. Diagnosis: --reserved-space /
  --keep-storage bounds the RECLAIMABLE cache, not the total; entries still
  in use by the running stack sit on top of the reserve. Reclaimable was
  5.193GB against a 5 GiB (~5.37e9 byte) reserve — the cap was in fact
  holding exactly as designed.
  Resolution: §6.3 corrected in the brief to assert the ceiling on
  reclaimable. PASSES as corrected. Operator-directed.

DEVIATION 2 — §6.4's premise was false about pre-existing behavior, and
ruling 2's wording rested on it. This was raised to the operator as a v36
HARD STOP before any workaround was attempted.
  §6.4 as written required the pip/requirements layer to show CACHED on a
  second consecutive deploy, calling this "the property the whole ruling
  rests on". It rebuilt. Diagnosis, in order:
    - deploy #2 (first swept, from the 26.86GB unswept cache): pip rebuilt.
    - deploy #3 (from the swept steady state): pip rebuilt — ruling out a
      cold-start artifact.
    - Two consecutive bare `make build` runs with NO prune between them:
      pip rebuilt BOTH times while the cache GREW 5.78GB -> 6.16GB.
      Decisive: the sweep is not the cause.
    - Root cause: the `build:` target runs
      `docker compose build --no-cache`. No build has ever read the BuildKit
      cache. The pip layer could never have been a cache hit, before or
      after this brief.
  The operator confirmed --no-cache is INTENTIONAL and needs no ticket
  (2026-08-15), and directed that ruling 2's wording be fixed.
  Resolution, committed in 45b9749 — brief §1 ruling 2, the Makefile comment
  and arch doc §2.2 all corrected to the same position:
    - The cap costs nothing in build time, and `-a` on the BUILDER prune
      would save nothing there either. The cap is kept as a conservative,
      free bound — not as a cache-preservation measure. The original clause
      ("the next deploy's expensive layer (pip) is still a cache hit") is
      struck.
    - The `-a` prohibition that IS genuinely load-bearing is on the IMAGE
      prune: `docker image prune -a` removes tagged base images and forces a
      cold re-pull of python:3.12-slim — the 2026-07-29 slowdown. §4.4.1 was
      correct all along and is unaffected.
    - §6.4 correspondingly re-aimed at the base-image property, which is
      real, observable and passes.
  What ships is byte-for-byte what §4 specified. Only the justification and
  the two checks changed.

Note for the record: --no-cache is also why the dev VM accrues ~2GB per
build — every build is a full rebuild by construction. Operator has ruled
this intentional; recorded as context only, not as an open item.
